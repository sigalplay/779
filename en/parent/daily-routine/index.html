How do you build and use a daily routine board for kids?

What is a daily routine board for kids?

A daily routine board for kids is a visual tool that uses pictures and words to show the activities expected throughout the day. Instead of relying only on verbal instructions, the child can see what to do now, what comes next, and what is expected afterward. Visual information stays in view and can be revisited at any point.

With the Let's Play builder, you can pick only the activities that fit your routine, arrange them in the order you want, and create a personal board that can be printed or shared to a phone. This way the board can suit a family, a preschool, or a clinic visit, without requiring a fixed schedule set in advance.

How do you build a routine board?
  1. Choose whether to build the board for a boy or a girl.
  2. Pick the relevant activities for your routine from the image library.
  3. Arrange the activities in the order they happen for you.
  4. Add a custom activity if the one you need isn't in the library.
  5. Print the board or create a link to the interactive board on a phone.

It's best to start with a short, clear sequence of three or four activities. Once the child is familiar with using the board, you can gradually add more activities as needed.

Why use a visual board?

A visual board can help children understand the order of activities, prepare for transitions between tasks, and take a more active part in getting ready. Knowing what is happening now and what will happen next can make the routine clearer and more predictable and reduce the need for repeated verbal reminders.

The board can be used regularly or only during times when getting ready is more complex, such as in the morning, before bedtime, before leaving the house, or ahead of a change in routine. The goal isn't to perform every step perfectly, but to create a clear visual framework the child can understand and check.

What situations can a board be used for?

You can build a board for the morning routine, getting ready for bed, heading to preschool or school, coming home, showering and getting dressed, packing a bag, getting ready for an appointment, and even for a schedule on vacation or the weekend.

For the start of the day, you can use the Morning Routine Board for Kids, and to end the day you can build an Evening Routine Board for Kids. To show activities, appointments, and events throughout the week, you can create a Weekly Routine Board for Kids.

Who is a routine board suitable for?

A routine board can suit young children, children who prefer visual information, and children who need clearer structure during the day. Parents, preschool teachers, and caregivers can adjust the number of steps, the type of activities, and the order of images to fit each child's age, ability, and routine.

The board doesn't need to be long or crowded. Sometimes a short sequence covering just the points where the child needs help will be clearer and more useful than a board showing every activity of the day.

How do you use the board together with a child?

It's a good idea to go over the board together before starting the routine and show the child which activity comes first. After finishing each activity, you can check it off and direct attention to the next one. Try using short, consistent phrases, such as: "We're done getting dressed, now let's brush our teeth."

Over time, you can encourage the child to check for themselves what the next step is. If the sequence changes, it's best to update the board and show the child the change in advance whenever possible. The board is a flexible tool, so feel free to adjust it to whatever works for you.
